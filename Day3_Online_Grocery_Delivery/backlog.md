# Product Vision, Scrum Roles and User Story Backlog

## 1. Product vision

For customers of a regional supermarket chain who want convenient grocery shopping, the Online Grocery Delivery Application is a simple digital platform that lets them discover products, place orders and follow deliveries from home. Unlike calling the store or visiting in person, it provides a clear catalogue, an easy checkout process and visible delivery updates.

## 2. Scrum roles and one anti-pattern each

| Scrum role | Main responsibility | One anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Product Owner | Maximises product value and orders the Product Backlog based on customer and business needs. | Acts as a secretary who accepts every request without prioritising. |
| Scrum Master | Helps the team understand Scrum and removes or helps resolve impediments. | Acts as a traditional manager who assigns every task and controls the team. |
| Developers | Build a usable, tested product Increment during each Sprint. | Work in isolated silos and hand work over without collaborating. |

## 3. Epics

- **EPIC-1 – Browse and Search:** Help customers discover grocery products.
- **EPIC-2 – Cart and Checkout:** Help customers prepare and place an order.
- **EPIC-3 – Delivery Tracking:** Help customers understand the progress of an order.

## 4. Product backlog – 12 user stories

## 5. Backlog ordering rationale

Must-have stories establish the smallest useful shopping journey: discover a product, add it to a cart, review the amount and select a delivery address. Should-have stories improve discovery and customer updates. The estimated delivery window is a useful enhancement, so it is Could-have for the initial release.

Priority is a business decision and story points are relative effort estimates; a higher priority does not automatically mean more points.
